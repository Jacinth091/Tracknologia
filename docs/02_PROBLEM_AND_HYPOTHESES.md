# 02 — Problem and Hypotheses

## Problem space

Tracknologia is testing whether repair work becomes unnecessarily difficult when repair information is fragmented across paper, chat, spreadsheets, memory, or informal coordination.

The product also tests whether customers experience avoidable uncertainty after handing a device to a repair provider.

## H1 — Provider organization problem

Repair Shops and Independent Repairers may have difficulty maintaining a reliable view of active repair jobs when they handle multiple customers or use fragmented records.

## H2 — Customer visibility problem

Customers may repeatedly call, message, or visit a Provider because they do not know the current repair state.

## H3 — Update-friction hypothesis

Providers will maintain digital repair records only if creating and updating a Repair is fast enough to fit their real workflow.

## H4 — Customer self-entry hypothesis

Some Customers, especially technologically comfortable ones, may prefer to describe their device and problem themselves through a Repair Request.

This must not become mandatory because other Customers may prefer verbal/walk-in intake.

## H5 — Independent-repairer hypothesis

Independent Repairers may gain particular value from:

- mobile-friendly direct Repair creation;
- provider-specific request links;
- digital repair tickets;
- customer tracking;
- flexible Service Modes;
- Service Area rather than mandatory public shop address.

## H6 — Meaningful-state hypothesis

Providers are more likely to maintain status when Tracknologia asks them to record only meaningful operational state changes, rather than every technical activity such as diagnosing, repairing, and testing.

## H7 — Request-to-Repair hypothesis

A customer-submitted Repair Request should remain separate from an accepted Repair so that:

- unaccepted inquiries do not pollute active repair records;
- the Provider can verify customer-supplied information;
- the Provider remains authoritative over official Repairs.

## Main validation question

> Will Repair Providers consistently manage real accepted Repairs in Tracknologia, and will Customers use the resulting tracking experience enough to reduce uncertainty or communication friction?
